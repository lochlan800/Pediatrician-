    What the job is actually like

    The best parts

    • Children get better fast. You see people go from very sick to running down the corridor.
    • You're trusted with the thing families care about most in the world.
    • Enormous variety — newborns, teenagers, emergencies, long-term conditions.
    • You genuinely never stop learning.
    • Silliness is part of the job. Stickers, jokes and daft voices are clinical tools.

    The hard parts (worth knowing early)

    • Some children don't get better. That stays with you, and you learn to carry it, not avoid it.
    • Night shifts, weekends and missed birthdays are real.
    • Training is long, and exams keep coming for years.
    • You're often managing frightened parents as much as the illness.
    • You have to be able to notice when a child is unsafe at home, and act on it.

    A typical hospital day

    • 08:00 Handover — the night team tells you about every child on the ward.
    • 08:30 Ward round with the consultant. Examine, discuss, plan, explain.
    • 11:00 Jobs: bloods, referrals, prescriptions, phone calls to GPs and parents.
    • 13:00 Lunch, in theory.
    • 14:00 Clinic or admissions — new children arriving through A&E.
    • 17:00 Handover to the evening team, and teaching if you're lucky.
